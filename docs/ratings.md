# Ratings

How the bot rates players: OpenSkill, placement, MVP and key-player awards, seasons, the NeatQueue
interim, the leaderboard and the calibration tools. The player-facing version is the wiki's
[Ratings](https://github.com/VARSITYLUNCHBUG/DayShift/wiki/Ratings).

## The model

OpenSkill Plackett-Luce (`league/domain/openskill_v1.py`). Each player has a skill estimate (mu,
starting at 25) and an uncertainty (sigma, starting at 8.33). After the library's update:

| Rule | Starting value |
|---|---|
| Placement: a player's first rated matches | 10 |
| During placement, a game moves mu by at most | 3 |
| MVP share of a win / of a loss | ×1.20 / ×0.80 |
| Key player share of a win / of a loss | ×1.10 / ×0.90 |
| Most an award moves one player's mu in a game | 0.5 |
| Awards apply | after placement |
| Sigma never drops below | 2 |
| Sigma grows, in quadrature, per 14 days without a game, up to 6 | 0.5 |

Awards are zero-sum within each team: the MVP and key players take a bigger share of their team's
update, and their teammates a smaller one, so a team's total change is what it would have been
without them.

### What players see

The shown rating is the conservative `max(0, mu − 3 × sigma) × 40`. A new player shows 0 and
climbs as the system becomes sure of them, and a high seed (`/rating seed band:high`, mu 35) also
starts low on purpose. Player lists show only the rating; `/stats` shows how many placement
matches a player has played.

| Tier | `mu − 3 × sigma` | Shown rating |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze | 0 | 0 |
| Silver | 10 | 400 |
| Gold | 18 | 720 |
| Platinum | 25 | 1000 |
| Diamond | 30 | 1200 |

Captain tie-breaks, the first player pick and auto-picks compare mu, not the shown rating, so a
strong player still in placement isn't treated as weak. After the player draft, each team heading
on the board shows its chance to win, with ⚠️ outside 40–60%. That is information only.

**Placement counts games, not sigma.** With 12 players a match, sigma shrinks slowly: in a
40-match synthetic league it was still about 7.1 after 24 games, from 8.33. So the cap and the
awards switch at `placement_games` rated matches instead.

## Seasons and parameters

All the values live in each season's `rating_params`, over the defaults, and are fixed once the
season has rated a match. Start a new season with fresh ratings with the **Start the next season**
action on the server's row in the admin, which asks for any new values as JSON. Or from a shell:

```bash
uv run python manage.py start_season --params '{"beta": 3.5}'
```

## Checking the values

The values are judgement, not measured. Check them on the [calibration dashboard](#calibration-dashboard),
or from a shell:

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `manage.py rating_calibration` | Brier score and log loss against always saying 50%, calibration bins and the Hidden King win rate, with 95% intervals |
| `manage.py rating_calibration --params '{"beta": 3.5}'` | Compares candidate parameters without writing anything |
| `manage.py rating_calibration --simulate 300` | Runs a synthetic league |

Run each with `uv run python`, or with `docker compose exec web python` against a running stack.

### Convergence, still to check before launch

In simulation a strong player seeded as new takes 29 to 81 games to reach mu 30. A 6v6 game moves
mu well under the 3-point placement cap, so the cap isn't what slows it: the per-game step is, and
`beta` barely changes it with 12 players. Seeding known players with `/rating seed` helps most. A
higher starting `sigma` helps a little (12 gave 15 to 72 games) but keeps shown ratings at 0 for
longer; try it with `rating_calibration --simulate 300 --params '{"sigma": 12}'`.

## The ledger

Ratings are a ledger, not a column. `rating_events` is append-only and enforced by database
triggers, and `player_ratings` is a projection that can be rebuilt from it. Rows carry exact
decimal `mu_delta` and `sigma_delta`, so the projection is the start plus the sum. A correction,
including awards that arrive late, appends differences and never rewrites history.

- `/match explain` shows the trail for a match.
- `manage.py check_ledger` (or **Check ledger** on the admin's Rating runs list) checks the
  projection against the ledger; `manage.py rebuild_ratings` recreates `player_ratings` from it,
  and never writes to the ledger itself.
- Migrations are generated by `makemigrations` and never edited: the triggers are installed after
  every `migrate` by `league/db_guards.py`.

Anything that rewrites past ratings shows a preview and needs Confirm.

## MVP and key players

Deadlock's scoreboard marks one MVP and two key players. They reach the rating in four ways, and a
later one replaces an earlier one only if it ranks at least as high:

1. **The report form.** Optional MVP and Key players pickers. The confirming captain's picks
   replace the reporter's. Awards never cause a dispute.
2. **[Score OCR](integrations.md#score-ocr)**, when it reads all three badges cleanly and has
   matched each to a player.
3. **[deadlock-api](integrations.md#result-checks)**, from the game's own MVP ranking, when all
   three are linked players.
4. **Staff**, with `/match awards`. Nothing replaces a staff decision except staff.

Before the result is final, awards are only recorded and the result is rated with them. After,
they go through the same refold as a rescore, and the results post is edited in place.

**MVP farming.** A player whose MVP rate, over 20 or more rated matches, is more than twice what
chance gives (1 in 12) keeps playing normally but gets no award share until the rate drops back.
Staff are pinged the first time. It is worked out from the same history on every replay, so a
later award correction can lift or add a freeze. The values are `farming_min_games`,
`farming_ratio` and `mvp_expected` in the season's parameters.

**Do awards mean anything?** The dashboard and `rating_calibration` compare how award holders do
in their next 5 matches against the rating's own prediction, next to everyone else. If award
holders keep beating it, awards carry skill the rating hasn't caught yet and the share could grow a
little. If the interval spans zero, keep it small, or set the multipliers to 1 to turn it off.

## NeatQueue interim

The server cuts over from NeatQueue, so players' NeatQueue points keep counting until staff switch
to OpenSkill. NeatQueue's all-time leaderboard export (`player_stats_alltime_leaderboard.csv`)
imports as it comes: the import reads its Player ID, MMR (rounded), Wins, Losses and Player Name
columns and ignores the rest. A CSV you write yourself works too:

```
discord_id,name,rating,wins,losses
123456789012345678,Kestrel,1240,40,22
```

In the admin, **Import NeatQueue CSV** on the Interim ratings list takes the file, shows a preview
(who is imported, the highest points, any lines it skipped) and imports on Confirm. The header may
name the columns (`discord_id`/`player_id`/`id`, `rating`/`points`/`mmr`, `wins`, `losses`, and
`name`/`player_name` optionally), or leave it out to read them in that order. IDs may be plain,
a mention, or wrapped as `="..."` the way NeatQueue writes them. Importing again replaces the earlier
import. Confirming turns the interim on.

While it's on, each completed match moves every player's points Elo-style on the two teams'
average points: a win gains `K × (1 − expected)` and a loss costs `K × expected`, so an even match
moves 16 with the default K of 32. Players who weren't in the import start at 1000. The
leaderboard channel, `/leaderboard`, `/stats`, the board and the results post show the points and
the NeatQueue record plus matches played here. OpenSkill keeps rating every match underneath and
still drives captain tie-breaks, the first player pick and auto-picks.

The points are rebuilt from the import and the season's completed matches on every result and
correction, so a rescore or a void puts them right by itself. Turning **Interim points** off in
[Runtime settings](admin.md#runtime-settings) is the cutover: players see the OpenSkill rating from
then on. The start points and K-factor are runtime settings; changing them rebuilds the points as
if they had always applied.

## Leaderboard

`#leaderboard` shows everyone in the league as images, highest first: rank, rating (points during
the interim), wins and losses, and win rate. Everyone in the league means anyone with a rating this
season, a NeatQueue import or any match here, so a player who hasn't played yet is listed at the
start rating; an admin login that never joined isn't.

Each image holds up to ten players, and players are shared out evenly, so 23 players are three
images of 8, 8 and 7. Every image is the same size. The bot redraws them after every result,
correction and rating change, and every hour, editing its messages in place: posting more if the
league grows and deleting any it no longer needs.

`/leaderboard` shows a player their own line, and `/stats` their place on the same table with their
full record. With a PUG role set, only that role sees the channel and can use the command.

The images use the brand's fonts, Figtree and Bricolage Grotesque, bundled in `bot/assets/fonts/`
under the SIL Open Font License.

## Calibration dashboard

`/admin/calibration/`, behind the Django staff login (the header's View site link goes there). It
shows, per season:

- Brier score and log loss against always saying 50%
- a calibration chart of predicted chance against how often it came true, with 95% intervals
- the favourite's and Hidden King's win rates
- how many players are still in placement, and the spread of uncertainty
- whether awards mean anything: how award holders do in their next 5 matches against the
  prediction, next to everyone else
- whose award share is frozen, and how many players left a game early
- awards by source, the most MVPs, score OCR reads and deadlock-api checks

Paste candidate parameters to see what they would have done. Every number is replayed in memory,
so nothing is written.
